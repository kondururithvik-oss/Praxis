Aim of the Experiment

To measure the energy band gap of a semiconductor material (Silicon/Germanium) using a p-n junction diode under reverse bias conditions.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand the concept of energy band structure (valence band, conduction band, and forbidden energy gap) in semiconductors.
  • Investigate the thermal generation of minority carriers in a p-n junction under reverse bias.
  • Log reverse saturation current ($I_0$) values across a wide temperature spectrum ($30^\circ\text{C}$ to $110^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Apply least-squares linear regression to plot $\ln(I_0)$ vs $1000/T$ and extract the experimental energy gap ($E_g$).
  • Contrast the band gap energies of Germanium and Silicon, identifying how they affect device performance and thermal operational limits.

Apparatus Required

The virtual experiment consists of the following instrumentation modules:

Regulated DC Supply (0-5V)
Digital Microammeter (0-1000µA)
Heater Oil Bath with Probe
Semiconductor Diode (Si/Ge)

Experimental Procedure

  1. Complete the trainer kit circuit connections by patching the wires:
    • Connect **VOLTS −** (Black terminal, top left) to **DIODE A** (Anode red socket, center daughter board).
    • Connect **DIODE K** (Cathode black socket, center daughter board) to **MICRO AMPS −** (Black terminal, bottom right).
    • Connect **MICRO AMPS +** (Red terminal, bottom right) to **VOLTS +** (Red terminal, top left) to complete the reverse bias loop.
    • Connect the two yellow fader **OUT PUT COIL** sockets to the yellow **COIL** sockets on the center daughter board.
  2. Turn ON the main power switch at the bottom left of the green PCB board. The LED display meters and red status light will light up.
  3. Choose your semiconductor diode material by selecting either Silicon (Si) or Germanium (Ge) under the selector card in the control panel.
  4. Turn ON the HEATER switch (metal toggle above the heater knob). Set a constant reverse bias voltage using the Reverse Bias Voltage slider on the control panel. Confirm that the VOLTS digital meter displays the chosen value (e.g., $1.50\text{ V}$).
  5. Begin the thermal sweep automatically by clicking the green-bordered ▶ Start Heating Sweep button. The heater coil will turn red and the oil bath beaker will begin to glow as the temperature steadily rises in $10^\circ\text{C}$ increments from $30^\circ\text{C}$ to $110^\circ\text{C}$.
  6. At each $10^\circ\text{C}$ step, the system will pause briefly for thermal stabilization, read the corresponding reverse saturation current ($I_0$) on the MICRO AMPS meter, and automatically write it to the observations log.
  7. If you prefer manual control, you can control the heating by toggling the **HEATER** switch. Wait for the reading to stabilize, and click **📍 Record Values** to manually log the current data point.
  8. After taking at least 3 temperature measurements, navigate to the Result tab and click 📐 Calculate Eg. This performs linear regression on the logged values ($\ln(I_0)$ vs $1000/T$) and animates the best-fit line to extract the energy band gap.
  9. Check the calculated $E_g$ against expected standard values ($1.10\text{ eV}$ for Si, $0.67\text{ eV}$ for Ge) and inspect the $R^2$ fit quality coefficient.

Theoretical Formulation

The reverse saturation current ($I_0$) flowing through a p-n junction diode under reverse bias is driven by minority carrier diffusion across the depletion zone. This current is modeled by the following temperature dependency equation:

I₀(T) = A · T³ · exp(−Eg / 2kT)

Where:

  • $I_0$: Reverse saturation leakage current in amperes.
  • $A$: Material-dependent pre-exponential constant.
  • $T$: Absolute temperature in Kelvin ($\text{K} = {^\circ\text{C}} + 273.15$).
  • $E_g$: Energy band gap energy of the semiconductor in electron-volts ($\text{eV}$).
  • $k$: Boltzmann constant = $8.617333 \times 10^{-5}\text{ eV/K}$.

Linearization & Graph Slope

Taking the natural logarithm on both sides yields:

ln(I₀) = ln(A · T³) − Eg / (2kT)

Since the $T^3$ term varies extremely slowly compared to the exponential term over the experimental temperature range ($30^\circ\text{C}$ to $110^\circ\text{C}$), the $\ln(A \cdot T^3)$ term behaves approximately as a constant. Plotting $\ln(I_0)$ vs $1000/T$ yields a straight line with slope $m$:

m = −Eg / (2000 · k) ⇒ Eg = −2000 · k · m

Measuring the slope $m$ of the linear fit line allows us to directly calculate the energy band gap $E_g$ of the semiconductor.
