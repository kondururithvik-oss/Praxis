📚 PN Junction Theory & Formulas

A P-N junction is formed by combining p-type and n-type semiconductor regions. The application of forward bias voltage lowers the potential barrier across the depletion layer, prompting majority carriers to diffuse and produce an exponential rise in current.

In reverse bias, the depletion layer widens, blocking major carrier movement and leaving only a negligible reverse saturation current (leakage current) to flow.

Shockley Diode Equation

Id = Is × [ eVd / (n × Vt) - 1 ]

Where:

  • Id: Diode current.
  • Is: Reverse saturation current (Silicon ≈ 10-12 A, Germanium ≈ 10-6 A).
  • Vd: Diode voltage.
  • n: Ideality factor (1.0 for Silicon, 1.2 for Germanium).
  • Vt: Thermal voltage (Vt ≈ 25.85 mV at 25°C room temperature).

PN Diode Theory

1. Basic PN Junction Physics

A P.N. junction is formed by joining p-type and n-type semiconductor materials. Conduction occurs rapidly after crossing the knee voltage (cut-in voltage) of ~0.7V for Silicon and ~0.3V for Germanium.

2. Shockley Diode Equation

The relationship between the voltage across the diode (Vd) and the current (Id) is governed by the Shockley equation:

Id = Is * ( eVd / (n * Vt) - 1 )

Where:

  • Is: Reverse Saturation Current. (Silicon ≈ 10-12 A, Germanium ≈ 10-6 A)
  • n: Ideality factor (1.0 for Si, 1.2 for Ge)
  • Vt: Thermal voltage. At room temp (25°C), Vt ≈ 25.85 mV

3. Patch Connection & Bias Polarity

In this analog simulation, you must build the circuit yourself. By connecting wires from the **DIODE PORT** to the Silicon or Germanium terminals, you insert the diode. - **Forward Bias**: Connect Port-Anode (+) to Diode Anode (left) and Port-Cathode (-) to Diode Cathode (right). - **Reverse Bias**: Reverse these connections (cross-wire) so Port-Anode (+) goes to Diode Cathode (right) and Port-Cathode (-) goes to Diode Anode (left).
